The River of Glass
by Wren Talbot

They called it glass because that's what it did to light, not because of
what it was made of. Nobody who'd waded the shallows of the Verre came
back certain what it was made of.

Osrin had crossed it twice before, both times at the narrows below
Auldwyn, both times with a rope and someone sober holding the other end.
This time there was no rope, no one sober, and the narrows were an hour's
walk in the wrong direction from the thing chasing them.

"Just don't stop moving," he told the boy. "Whatever you see under there,
it isn't looking at you back."

That was a lie, or at least Osrin hoped it was one. The stories disagreed.

The water took their ankles like cold glass indeed, sharp-edged and
strange, and something vast and slow turned beneath the surface without
ever quite breaking it. The boy made a sound and Osrin gripped his collar
and didn't let either of them look down again until the far bank came up
soft and muddy and blessedly ordinary under their hands.

Behind them, on the near shore, their pursuers had stopped at the water's
edge. Whatever else could be said about the River of Glass, it kept its
own counsel about who it let pass, and it had apparently decided, tonight,
that it liked the boy's odds better than the men with knives.

"We're not doing that again," the boy said, teeth chattering.

"No," Osrin agreed, already scanning upstream for the lights of Fenmarch.
"Once is a story. Twice is a bad habit."
